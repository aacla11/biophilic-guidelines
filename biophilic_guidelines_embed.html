Biophilic Design Guidelines
for Residential Housing

Evidence-based strategies that integrate nature into homes — cost-effectively compared to standard builder-grade construction.

For architects  ·  interior designers  ·  developers

Biophilic design is grounded in the science of how humans relate to the natural world. These foundational principles, drawn from the Kellert et al. (2008) framework, organize every strategy in this guidebook.

Category 01 · Direct Experience of Nature

Contact with living systems and natural forces

Direct encounters with nature — natural light, air movement, water, vegetation, and weather — provide the most powerful biophilic stimulus. In residential design, this category encompasses daylighting strategies, operable windows, indoor plants, living walls, water features, and connections to outdoor gardens. Research consistently links direct nature contact to reduced stress, improved mood, and faster physiological recovery.

Category 02 · Indirect Experience of Nature

Representation of nature in materials, form, and pattern

Indirect biophilic experience occurs through natural materials, organic patterns, imagery of nature, and the visual quality of age and change in materials. Wood, stone, rattan, linen, and clay — and design patterns echoing natural geometries (fractals, spirals, flowing curves) — activate biophilic responses even in the absence of living systems. This category is often the most cost-accessible entry point for residential biophilic design.

Category 03 · Experience of Space and Place

Spatial configurations that echo natural environments

Humans evolved preferences for certain spatial configurations — the ability to see without being seen (prospect and refuge), variable ceiling heights, layered spaces, and elements of mystery. These qualities can be designed into residential environments through varied ceiling heights, alcoves, semi-enclosed niches, thresholds, and orchestrated views. They are architectural moves, not material additions, and carry no additional construction cost when integrated at the design stage.
